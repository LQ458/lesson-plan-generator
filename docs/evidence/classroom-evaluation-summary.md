# Classroom evaluation summary

This document retains aggregate classroom context without student-level
records.

## Preserved comparison

The reviewed workbook contains 100 raw post-lesson records across two classes:
50 in the AI-material class and 50 in the comparison class. Missing values
produce different usable samples by subject:

| Outcome | AI-material class | Comparison class | Total usable |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Chinese | 49 | 48 | 97 |
| Mathematics | 49 | 50 | 99 |
| English | 49 | 49 | 98 |
| Complete across all three | 49 | 48 | 97 |

The values 98 and 99 in saved reports therefore describe different
subject-specific or analysis conventions. The preserved workbook has no stable
student identifier that supports describing 98 observations as a matched or
paired sample.

A wider project reach of approximately 150 students across classes is
project-lead-confirmed. An independent supporting artifact for that exact total
was not preserved in the reviewed materials, so it is not a
repository-verified metric.

## Design

- Assignment occurred by existing class: one class used AI-generated materials
  and one class used comparison materials.
- Students were not individually randomized.
- The design is quasi-experimental and has one class per condition.
- Class, teacher, review time, test difficulty, and administration differences
  are plausible confounders.
- Saved notes indicate that the comparison group may have received an easier
  English assessment and additional review time.
- A delayed-test administration error was documented and corrected.
- Results varied by subject.

## Interpretation

The preserved materials support the statement:

> System-generated teaching materials were evaluated in a two-class classroom
> comparison with 100 raw records and 97–99 usable observations depending on
> subject.

They do not support claims of a randomized controlled trial, proven causal
improvement, uniform gains across subjects, production use by 150 accounts, or
verified reductions in teacher preparation time.

Some reports raised concerns about repeated discrete scores, while a later
review argued that repeated values were expected for limited score ranges.
Without de-identified answer-level audit evidence, that disagreement should not
be presented as a resolved independence or misconduct finding.

## Recommended future evidence

Future evaluation should define a de-identified row key, missing-data rules, and
subject-specific analysis sets before analysis. More than one class per
condition, equivalent assessments, consistent review time, and documented
administration would reduce the current confounding.
